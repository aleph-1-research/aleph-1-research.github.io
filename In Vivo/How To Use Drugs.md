### Set and Setting

Drugs are mysterious creatures.  When we consume a drug there is a tendency to believe that a because the drug is the same, the experience will be the same.  But during each experience you are different.  You're body, you're mind, and location in space is all different.  Drugs interact with you as are you in this ever changing moment.  So, when doing drugs it is helpful to start by attending yourself, your body, your mind, how you feel, and where you are in space.  Do you feel safe?  All of this will change your experience.

### Dosage

Measuring is helpful. The experience we have on a drugs is in proportion to, but not necessary linear with the dosage we do.  For example, the difference between one and two hits of 50 µg acid is pretty negligible.  The experience is stronger but not radically different.  However at some point between 4-6 hits, and depending on set and setting of course, the experience makes a jump to another level.  At this dosage I am very likely to spending at least an hour or two laying down with my eyes closed, having an internal experience.  This pattern is true with many drugs, through the next level of the experience varies greatly depending on the chemical.  It is a fast track to having a bad time if you induce this experience within yourself accidentally. So be careful, attend your dosage, and as you explore higher dosages beware of the risks.

##### Safety Ratio

One thing to be aware of when dosing a drug is the safety ratio or therapeutic index.  This is the ratio between the effective dose and the lethal dose.  The smaller that ratio is, the more dangerous.

###### A Protocol for New Drugs & New Batches

Every batch of a drug is different.  The specific concentration of the active compound can vary widely.  For me there are two ways to manage that. If available, I try to use a concentration test on the drug.  This is available for LSD, cocaine, MDMA, and other drugs through [Bunk Police](https://bunkpolice.com/product-category/qtest-purity-test-kits/?_gl=1*jdeli0*_up*MQ..*_gs*MQ..&gclid=Cj0KCQjwkt_UBhDMARIsALpnOAzvVdfXZcUrYj-k29Rd3noXMyfDMEEjHqAn13kM9qct-djnOJNfkRMaAqgrEALw_wcB&gbraid=0AAAAAC4Z792hIwSDZkpKt6PEwsPVl3QJ2). 

Alternatively, when I first doing a drug from a new batch and I'm uncertain how strong it is I start by doing a "standard unit".  This might be 1 tab of [[Allies/LSD]], a 60 µg bump of [[Allies/Ketamine|Ketamine]], etc... It depends on the drug, and the exactly amount isn't important. What's important is that it's relatively low, and that you have expectations about what the experience will be like.  Once you then have the experience, you can calibrate yourself.  Is the experience more powerful than you expected? Less?  That can give some sense of how to dose this specific batch.

Now what if you haven't done the drug before?  I first start with [[Resources/PsychonautWiki]].  It provides standard dosages for most drugs you're likely to interact with.  For a first experience I tend to choose a dosage in the common range. One thing to keep in mind is that our bodies respond to drugs differently, and it is possibly for your body to be more or less sensitive to the drug relative to what is recommended.  So I like to start by taking a fraction of the dosage I intend to do. I'll let me body ingest that and wait enough time that onset begins to occur, and I will see if my body is reacting very strongly to what should be a very low dosage.  If my body doesn't pop off, then I assume that I'm probably not hypersensitive to the drug and I proceed with consuming the rest of the dosage.

If you're given a drug and you either don't know its name or you cannot find it on [[Resources/PsychonautWiki]], proceed with caution.  How would you dose such a drug?  [[Resources/Sasha Shulgin]] once offered a protocol for such a situation. You basically need to do as little as possible.  Consider that [[Fentanyl]] is effective at 5 µg. So if you assume the drug is effective at the mg range and it turns out to be [[Fentanyl]]... You've killed yourself. So in this situation you need to start incredibly small.  Pretend its [[Fentanyl]], and dose yourself accordingly. If we're being honest, I think this protocol is probably more advanced that most people are prepared for, and I really don't recommend anyone do this unless they understand the risks.

### Testing

Drug testing new batches of chemicals is incredibly important.  If you aren't buying enough that you have some to spare to test, then you need to ask your dealer what they did.  In either case there are three things that are good to test for.

Test for:
- The presence of the drug you're looking for
- The presence of the drugs you're not looking for
- Concentration of the drug you're looking for if possible

What exactly you should test for depends on the chemical.  Vendors like [DanceSafe](https://dancesafe.org/) and [BunkPolice](https://bunkpolice.com/) offer testing kits for a variety of chemicals.
### Harm Reduction

This is a hard topic and is very specific to the chemical you're interacting with.  I would say in general, try to understand how a drug is damaging to the body.  Provide support to your body where you can, and be aware that in every case doing a drug all the time is destructive to your body.  Finding balance with a drug is a core part of harm reduction. I give more specific advice for managing harm reduction in my discussion of individual chemicals later in this book.

### Tolerance

Sometimes we use a drug regularly, get used to certain level of dosage, and then take a break.  When returning to the drug, recalibration is required. Don't assume you can do the same amount you used to.  This can be very dangerous, and is a good way to overdose particularly with drugs that have lower safety ratios. 
### Addiction & Compulsive Redosing

Some time ago I [watched a lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOSD9rTVuWc) by [[Marc Lewis]] based on their book called [[Resources/The Biology of Desire]] which completely changed how I think about addiction. After watching the lecture I read the book, and have found in my life that it has provided the most useful model of addiction that I've encountered. In this section I will provide some thoughts on how I currently think about addiction highly informed by Marc's work.

It is important to say that this model doesn't just apply to drugs.  This model applies to any behavior which induces pleasure in the body (or really any behavior at all).  

When we first do an activity we have to choose to do it. Whether that's a novel chemical, watching porn, gambling, etc...  When you do it for the first time, you are making a choice to do it.  I like to call this an intentional behavior.  

However, after you've done it a couple times and find that you like it you will find that something else start to happen. The brain will prompt you to do the behavior.  A little voice will emerge in your awareness and say "oh, your remember that thing? It was so fun! Let's do it again!"  There are many ways that it might prompt you.  Sometimes with pictures or with words.  The feeling that underlies that prompting is desire.  But notice that you are still fully in control.  Desire is surfacing this idea within your executive function, which leaves you able to say either yes or no. 

When we are at this stage, this is called a habit.  And habits aren't necessarily bad.  Every morning, within an hour or so of waking up, my brain prompts me to make a cup of coffee.  And I love coffee! It fits perfectly into my life style, so I don't have any issue with this.  I have lots of habits that I feel are beneficial in my life, and many of the drugs that I do are in the habit stage.

When we say yes to a behavior all/most of the time the experience will begin to change.  In this stage, we find that desire stops surfacing in executive function, and instead triggers goal directed behavior instead.  When we are in this stage it is called a compulsion.  

It is important to note that not all compulsions are bad.  When you sneeze, it is very likely that you automatically cover your mouth in some way.  You are very likely doing this compulsively.  Compulsion are really a shortcut that the brain has established in order to make a certain behavior more efficient. By removing the need for you to make a decision, you can do the behavior quicker such as with a sneeze where you have very limited reaction time between the sensation of the sneeze about to occur and the sneeze itself.

However, when it comes to drugs compulsions can be pretty tough. Compulsively using a drug is how we ignore harm reduction, do the drug all the time, damage our bodies, and ultimately find us on a downward spiral towards rock bottom.  

I have compulsive behaviors with [[Allies/Nicotine]] and [[Allies/Cannabis]], and learning how to move these back to the habit stage has been some of the hardest work in my. life.  To accomplish it I needed a strategy.  In both cases, my strategy involves creating work.  
With [[Allies/Cannabis]] I went from buying large amounts, .5-1 oz, at a time to buying 3.5 g. I would only buy it when I had an event that I wanted it for, whether that was a friend coming over or a party where I would want it.  And then I would let myself do the drug.  

I would still try not to do it all the time.  I would try to notice the ways that if I did it earlier in the day, it would cause me to fall asleep or other wise have a less productive day.  Noticing the ways that a drug doesn't align with my goals, particularly when I using it all day, was a very helpful way for me to find motivation to resist it. 

Once the drug ran out, I wouldn't go get more.  I would wait until the next thing, and the fact that it wasn't in the home created this amount of work necessary to get more.  That work is actually essential, because it means I can't instantly gratify my desire. Instead it takes time, and during that time I have found that I can clock that I am experiencing desire and then practice patience.  For me, when I notice that I am experiencing desire I practice mindful breathing.  In the same way that one days in meditation, I simply return my attention to the breath. And each time my mind refocuses on desire, and I simply notice and then return my attention to the breath. You can do any activity though.  Some people stretch, some people walk.  The specific activity doesn't matter.  Just give yourself something to do while you're experiencing desire, and eventually the desire with pass.  Desire is impermanent, just like any feeling. 

Over time, I have found that cannabis has slipped back into the habit stage. It is taken time, patience, and giving myself the grace to make mistakes.  There is an effect that Marc discusses called Ego Fatigue.  The brain only has so much energy to resist a tempting behavior, and so if you find that you can no longer resist and you do a behavior even though you had intended not to it is important to forgive yourself.  You are not a failure because it happened, and it is not a sign that changing your relationship with the behavior is impossible. It's hard.

##### Compulsive Redosing

Some drugs induce a specific effect called compulsive redosing.  [[Allies/Nicotine]], [[Allies/Nitrous Oxide]], [[Allies/Ketamine]], [[Allies/Cocaine]], etc... There are a bunch, and interacting with drugs that have this property require a certain degree of skill to use them safely.  For me, I like to create a container when I'm doing a drug like this.  

I first developed my strategy with Nitrous.  I found that when I sat down for a Nitrous session, that I would do all of it. No matter the amount.  If I gave myself 4 boxes, and I would do all of them.  And so, I introduced a system to manage this.  Instead of giving myself my entire stash to use during the session, I would pull out the amount I wanted to do, and leave the rest somewhere else.  Doesn't really matter where, just far enough away that I can't reach over and do it mid session.  And then I would do the box.  

At the end of the box there is this work needed to continue, and this gives me just enough time to be able to notice that I am experiencing desire, and then say to myself "thank you, I'm done."  I will use the same behavior I described before of practicing mindful breathing, or doing any activity at all that isn't doing more of the drug and wait for desire to pass.  

The precise strategy that I use varies by the drug. I go into detail on my specific strategy in the section dedication to specific chemicals. 
### Accepting Impermanence

A final note.  I have had many magical experiences on drugs, and consequently have often felt the desire to chase the dragon. It is a desire that drives me to consume ever more.  I have had to very intentionally let go of this desire, and come to accept the truth about drug experiences.

Every experience is different.  Drug experiences are never fully predictable, and you will never recapture that experience you hunger for.  Embrace the impermanence of every experience. Each one is novel, new, fresh.  Embrace that truth. Let the incredible experiences you've had rest in your memory, but never try to recapture them.  Instead look forward to all of the new experiences you can have.
